KRA 2026 Tax Amnesty: What Your Business Needs to Know Before December 31

Brezoid Advisory Team July 2026 4 min read

The Finance Act 2026 introduced a tax amnesty framework giving Kenyan taxpayers until December 31, 2026 to clear historical penalties and interest on tax liabilities that arose on or before December 31, 2025. If your business has outstanding KRA obligations from prior years, this window, and the conditions attached to it, deserve your immediate attention.

The Amnesty Window

The relief applies to accumulated penalties, interest, and non-compliance fines attached to tax liabilities incurred before the end of 2025. It runs until December 31, 2026, after which unresolved penalties and interest revert to being enforceable in full.

Importantly, the waiver itself is automatic once the qualifying conditions are met: there is no separate application required to have penalties and interest written off. What KRA does require is that the underlying tax debt be settled correctly, which is where most businesses run into trouble.

The 100% Principal Rule

Qualifying for the automatic waiver hinges on one non-negotiable condition: the full principal tax owed must be paid in full.

The Rule That Matters Most

To trigger the waiver on penalties and interest, you must pay 100% of the underlying principal tax. Partial payments do not unlock a partial waiver: the relief only activates once the principal is cleared in full.

Businesses that cannot raise the full principal upfront are not without options. KRA's Automated Payment Plan (APP), accessible through the iTax Portal under the Debt and Enforcement tab, allows structured repayment. To keep the amnesty active while on a payment plan, you must submit a signed commitment letter and adhere strictly to the agreed schedule: a missed instalment can jeopardise the waiver.

Revised Filing Timelines

The Finance Act 2026 also tightened statutory filing deadlines, which businesses should factor into their compliance calendar alongside the amnesty process.

Key Filing Deadlines
Nil Returns Must be filed within 1 month after the end of the year of income.
Income Fully Taxed at Source (PAYE) Must be filed within 4 months after year-end.
Annual 2025 Returns Hard deadline was June 30, 2026, with no extensions granted. Late filers incurred a KSh 2,000 penalty, which can itself be waived under the 2026 amnesty once the taxpayer regularises their position.

The eTIMS Compliance Caveat

Before making any amnesty payment, businesses should be aware of a change that can quietly inflate their principal tax debt.

Audit Your Expenses First

From the 2026 year of income, KRA requires all business expenses to be supported by valid electronic tax invoices generated through eTIMS. Expenses without a matching eTIMS invoice are treated as taxable profit, which increases your principal tax liability before any amnesty calculation is applied.

We recommend auditing your expense records now, before committing to an amnesty payment, so you are settling the correct principal figure rather than an inflated one.

The amnesty is a genuine opportunity to clear the slate on historical non-compliance, but it rewards businesses that move early and verify their numbers before paying. Waiting until close to the December deadline leaves little room to correct errors in your principal balance or negotiate a payment plan.
